# TUI

Code: `ui/`, `app/`

- **Two views**, switched by the tabs on the top border of the list box (click or `Tab`): **Processes** (graphs of the last hour over the usage tree as a table: name, a memory bar, memory, CPU %, a CPU sparkline of the last minute, and for panes context/agent/status) and **Context** (the list of every Claude agent of the server on the left and what fills the marked one's context on the right, under a band with how full it is). Every part is a rounded box with its title and figures on its top border; the Processes box carries the keys on its bottom border, and on its right a newer version when there is one ([release check](release.md)). A box too narrow for both tabs shows only the active one, so the tab says which view is on screen. Rejected: a tab bar line of its own, which took a line from the table for two words.
- **The band on top is the same in both views**: the top lines keep the height of the graphs (three lines, four from 30 lines high, none under 16 lines or 60 columns), decided once for both views, so switching tabs moves no line whose content does not change (a test checks that every box border of Processes stays where it was in Context). In Processes it holds the graphs; in Context, the **marked agent**: its place, agent, model and status as the title; `Context used N of W tokens` and the % of the window (bold, in the level colour); a bar of the window, loaded on every message (`▓`) and this conversation (`▒`) in the level colour and free (`░`, gray), on its own line when the band has two inner lines and beside the words when it has one; and the legend in words (`loaded on every message`, `this conversation`, `free`, with their tokens) on its bottom border, each piece whole or not at all. Without the window only the total shows, with no bar (a full bar would read as a full context) and the legend without its symbols, without a total the use is unknown until Claude's next reply, and without a session `Context used —`. When the band is hidden, the conversation box carries `N of W · P%` on the right of its top border, like the list box carries the totals in Processes. Rejected: a bar and legend inside the Context box under a band-less top, which made every box jump when switching tabs.
- **Level colours**: a context % is green under 50 %, yellow under 80 % and red from 80 %, the same in the band, the agents list and the Processes `Context` column, so a full agent reads the same everywhere.
- **Context layout**: under the band, on the left, **agents**: every pane whose cornercase agent is `claude`, by a readable name, a bar and cornercase's context % in the level colour, the fullest first (a pane without a % last, ties in the tree's order), and `sorted by context used` at the foot when there is room. The name is `project › workspace`, plus the tab's name when it has one of its own (not the agent's or program's name, which cornercase gives new tabs, nor the project's or workspace's); the pane id (` %id`) is added only to names that would read the same. A name that does not fit drops the project (keeping the workspace, its tab and ` %id`), then is cut at its start, so a cut never leaves a piece of the project. The list shows no tokens: only the marked agent's transcript is read, and turning another agent's % into tokens would need its window, which would be a guess. The list is a third of the width (30 to 44 columns) and scrolls only to keep the marked agent on screen; under 70 columns it goes above the conversation with a third of the height at most. On the right, **what fills this conversation (N)**, since the last compaction, by group: `Commands`, `Files`, `Searches`, `Claude's replies`, `Your messages`, `Subagent results`, `Other tools`, from the largest down. Each group shows its tokens, its % of the conversation and a bar against the conversation (not against the largest group, so a bar says how much of the conversation it is); commands, files and searches open to their three largest parts and `… N more` with the tokens of the rest, the others are one figure. The two largest groups with parts start open and the rest folded; a click on a group, or `Enter` for all of them (it opens all when one is folded, folds all otherwise; the box's foot offers `⏎` only when there is a session), changes that, and the choice lasts until another agent is marked. Paths inside the pane's folder show relative and are cut at the start, so the file name stays; commands are joined to one line. An empty conversation shows `—`. At the bottom of the box, under a separator `loaded on every message (M)`, the loaded parts in one line from the largest down (wrapped, never cut mid-piece unless one alone is wider than the box), then the advice: `/compact would free about N; the M stays.` and, when one part of commands, files or searches takes over a tenth of the conversation, names it (`One command, … took N.` with the part in backticks; only the largest, and only figures from the breakdown); last, one note that the split is estimated and the total is Claude's own, or, without a total, that the parts are estimates. That bottom part keeps three lines for the groups and is cut from its end when the box is short. Only the groups scroll (the wheel, three lines); the scroll is clamped to the last group on screen and reset, with the folds, when the marked agent changes. Rejected: `≈` on every line, replaced by the one note; the words fixed and dynamic, which said nothing to a user; cornercase's % beside the total (below).
- **Which agent is marked**: entering Context (the tab, `Tab` or a double click) marks the Claude under the Processes selection: the pane, the pane of a selected process, or the fullest Claude in a selected tab, workspace or project; when the selection has none, or nothing is selected, the fullest agent. A click on the list or `↑`/`↓` marks another. A marked agent that leaves the list hands the mark to the fullest. A double click (two clicks on the same row within 400 ms) on a Processes row with a Claude under it opens Context with that agent; on a row without one it is just two clicks. Only the marked agent's transcript is read; the list uses cornercase's %, which is already in `status`. Rejected: keeping the agent marked last time when entering again, since the selection in Processes says better what the user is looking at.
- **The window**: no file holds the context window and cornercase only gives its rounded % (and a model alias such as `opus`), so the % shown is the total over whichever of Claude's two windows (200k, 1M) lands closer to cornercase's %, among those the total fits in; without cornercase's % only the total shows. The two windows are five times apart, so a total that is off still shows as a different %. cornercase's % is shown only in the agents list and the Processes `Context` column, never beside the total: it comes from Claude's status line, rounded and refreshed at its own pace, so two %s a few points apart next to each other read as an error when they are not. Rejected: copying Claude's window rule (model versions, settings, environment), which cornercase already mirrors and which drifts with every Claude release.
- **Empty states** of Context, each with its text in the conversation box (and `Context used —` in the band): no Claude agent in cornercase's panes (`No Claude agent…`, and `—` in the list), the context not read yet (`Reading the Claude session of …`), a pane without a `claude` process, and a `claude` whose session or transcript is not found (`No Claude session found…`, also when there is no config folder).
- **Mouse first**, like cornercase: click selects a row and folds or unfolds it on its arrow, a double click on a row with a Claude opens its Context, a header click sorts by that column, a click on an agent marks it, the wheel scrolls. Keys exist too, since the pane is cctop's own: arrows, `Enter` (fold), `Tab`, `s`, `k`, `q` and `Esc`. Capturing the mouse makes the terminal report every movement, even without a button pressed; those are dropped before they reach the app, so moving the pointer over cctop neither redraws nor does any work.
- **Refresh** every `interval` ([config](config.md)) on a background thread that samples and asks cornercase; the UI loop never blocks on I/O. Context is only read for one pane: the marked agent while Context is on screen, and while Processes is, the Claude under the selection (none when the selection has no Claude or nothing is selected, so an idle Processes view reads no transcript). The app sends that pane (or none) to the sampler over a channel whenever it changes, and the sampler answers at once with the new pane's context alone, read against its last sample, so switching panes does not wait for the next tick (a burst of selections is read once, for the last one). That answer is not a tick: it leaves the tree, the error and the time of cornercase's last answer alone, so a click while cornercase is down neither hides the error nor makes an old tree look fresh. A selection that moves without input (the selected process went away) is asked for on the next draw. Each tick reads that pane's transcript again (a `stat` when it did not grow), also while Processes is on screen, so switching to Context finds it ready. A context that belongs to another pane than the marked one shows as being read.
- **`status` has its own pace**: the sampler reads the processes every `interval` but asks cornercase's `status --json` only every 10 s (or every tick when `interval` is longer), and at once when a process of this server names a pane the last answer did not list (a new pane); pane ids that were already unknown when it last asked (a process that outlived its pane) do not ask again. A failed `status` is asked again on the next tick; if cornercase stops between two answers, nothing says so for up to 10 s and the tree keeps the last snapshot with fresh processes. A snapshot without `boot` places no process, so a new pane does not ask again early and waits for the 10 s. Between answers the tick reuses the last snapshot, so a new process shows in its pane within one `interval`, while an agent's status, its context % or a closed pane can lag up to 10 s. Each `status` costs about 45 ms of CPU in the `cornercase` child (it starts a whole binary) plus a few ms parsing its JSON here, which every 2 s alone was over 2 % of a core. Not tried: `cornercase events`, a stream that would say when to ask, at the price of a long-lived child to supervise.
- **Drawing only on change**: the loop redraws after a reading or a context arrives, after an input event (a resize included) and when the "has not answered" message changes its seconds; a poll that times out with nothing new draws nothing.
- **Cost at rest** (measured 2026-10-21, same machine and panes as in [processes](processes.md), the TUI in a pseudo-terminal for 60 s, its `/proc/<pid>/stat` time with the reaped children, several builds at once so they share the load): 3.1 to 4.1 % of a core before; 0.9 to 1.2 % after the changes above (sampler thread about 1 %, UI thread 0.15 %, `cornercase` children 0.2 %, run by run). The <1 % of [IDEA](../docs/IDEA.md) is for about 30 panes and this was 58 with 126 processes; the sample grows with the user's processes, so at about half of them it should fall under 1 %, which is not measured yet. Measured again after the graphs, bars and agents list (2026-10-22, same machine with 63 panes and about 160 processes of the user, the method of [VALIDATION](../docs/VALIDATION.md) §4, this build and the one before it side by side in two pseudo-terminals of 120×40 for three runs of 60 s): Processes untouched, 1.38 to 1.45 % against 1.60 to 1.81 % for the build before, the same within the noise; with Context on screen following a session of 449k tokens, 1.98 % against 1.91 % for the build before on Processes in the same minute. The history costs one push per row and reading, and a draw resamples the hour into the graphs' width, a few thousand additions, only when something changed. An idle Processes view reads no transcript unless the selection holds a Claude, as before. Measured again after the shared band and the grouped Context (2026-10-23, this build and the one before side by side in two pseudo-terminals of 120×40, 60 s with Context on screen following the fullest agent, about 210k tokens): 1.00 % of a core for both; the groups are a pass over the parts already in the breakdown on each draw.
- **Killing** (`k`, or a right click on the row, which also selects it) asks first in a dialog with the process, its pid, its group and its pane, and buttons `Cancel`, `Process` (focused) and `Group`; it sends `SIGTERM` to the process or to its group. Never without confirming. `Enter` presses the focused button, `←`/`→` and `Tab` move the focus, `Esc` cancels, a click presses a button; while the dialog is open no other key or click reaches the list (only `Ctrl-C` still quits), and `ui` returns only the buttons as hit areas.
- **What can be killed**: only process rows, never a project, workspace, tab or pane row. Never cctop itself nor a process in cctop's process group (that group holds the `cornercase` children the sampler runs, and killing it would take cctop down); cctop's group comes from `getpgrp`, not from finding itself in the sample, and if it is unknown nothing is offered. Asking for one shows a warning where the totals go and opens no dialog. On confirming, the process must still be in the latest sample with the same start time and group, is checked again against cctop's group, and the killer reads it once more right before the signal: a process that ended, changed group or whose pid was reused is not signalled and a warning says it ended. A failed signal (`EPERM`, …) shows its error in the same place. Rejected: `SIGKILL` or a second, stronger step, since `SIGTERM` lets the process clean up and the user can ask again.
- `ui` renders a `View` (plain data built by `app`) and returns hit areas for the mouse, so it is tested with snapshots and no I/O.
- **Processes layout**: on top, two boxes side by side, **cpu** and **mem**, each a braille graph of the last hour with its current value on the top border (`cpu` the tree's CPU as a share of the whole machine, the CPU % of every process over the number of CPUs; `mem` the tree's memory over the machine's, `used / total`) and `-60m` on the bottom border. The two `cpu` figures use different scales on purpose: the box's is a share of the machine (`40%` with 8 CPUs), while the rows' `CPU` and the list box's `cpu` total (shown when the graphs are hidden) add up per core (`314%`), like `top`, so a pane's figure reads as cores it keeps busy. Each column of the graph is two half cells of four dots and is green, yellow or red by its share (under 50 %, under 80 %, above); `mem` scales to the machine's memory, or to its own peak when that is unknown. The graphs take three lines (one of braille), four from 30 lines high, and are left out under 16 lines or 60 columns, before the table loses a line. Below, the **list box**: the tabs on its top border (`Processes`, `Context`; the active one reversed and bold), then the last error (red), or the totals (`mem`, `cpu`) when the graphs are hidden; a total that does not fit is left out whole (`cpu` first, then `mem`), never cut, since a cut number reads as a different one. Inside, a bold header of clickable columns `Name`, `Memory`, `CPU`, `CPU 60s`, `Context`, `Agent`, `Status`, the sorted one marked `▼`; then one line per visible row. Numbers are right-aligned, text left-aligned, one blank column between columns. The other columns take fixed widths and are added in this order while `Name` keeps its minimum: `Memory` (9), `CPU` (6), the memory bar (12) and `CPU 60s` (12) while `Name` keeps 16 columns, then `Context` (9), `Agent` (8) and `Status` (8) while it keeps 30, so the graphics come before the pane's text columns and at 80 columns names are still readable; at 40 columns only name, memory and CPU show. A pane's context % joins its name (`%3 claude · 42%`) when the `Context` column is not shown. `Name` takes the rest. The bottom border lists the keys, as many whole as fit. Rejected: ratatui's `Table`, because the mouse needs the same column rectangles the render uses; a graph per CPU core or of the whole machine's load, which belongs to btop and not to what cornercase costs.
- **Rows**: indented two columns per level, then `▾` (open), `▸` (folded) or nothing, then the label; anything too long is cut at the end with `…` and never moves the columns. The arrow's two cells fold, the rest of the line selects. Projects are bold, workspaces cyan, tabs blue, panes and processes plain; the hot path is yellow; the selected row is reversed across the box (reverse works on any terminal theme). Missing values show `—`, estimated memory `≈`. Context, Agent and Status are blank on every row that is not a pane, so `—` only means a pane without that value.
- **Memory bar**: the row's memory as a share of the tree's total, in eighths of a cell over a gray `░` track, so rows compare across levels and a project's bar says how much of what cornercase costs it holds. Its colour compares the row's memory with `memory_threshold` ([config](config.md)): green under half of it, yellow under it, red at or over it, the same line the alerts use for a pane. Rejected: a bar against the largest row of its level, which made a 30 MB process look as heavy as a 4 GB one.
- **CPU sparkline**: the row's CPU (its subtree, like the `CPU` column) over the last 60 s, one cell per slice of the minute, the newest on the right, averaged when a slice holds several readings; eight levels, the top one being 100 % of a core or the row's own peak when higher, and each cell green, yellow or red under 50 %, under 100 % or above one core. No reading yet shows blank.
- **History**: kept in memory only, fed by each reading the sampler already makes (a failed one adds nothing, nor does a re-sort). Per row, by its stable id, the CPU of the last 60 s (`60 s / interval` points, 30 at 2 s); for the tree, CPU and memory of the last hour (`3600 s / interval` points, 1,800 at 2 s), at most 3,600 points each whatever the interval. A row missing from a reading is forgotten, and comes back empty, so the history holds at most the rows on screen times their points. The graphs fold it into their width when drawing (two points per cell for braille), and a draw copies the minute of the rows on screen only. A reading without a CPU figure (a process's first sample) is kept as no reading, left out of its slice's average, so a slice holding only those stays blank. Rejected: persisting it, which would need a state file and gives little for an hour.
- **Stable row ids**: selection and folds are kept by id (`Project`, `Workspace`, `Tab` and `Pane` by cornercase's id, a process by pane and pid), never by line number, so they survive every refresh and re-sort. A selected row that disappears (its process ended) hands the selection to the row now at its place. Rows carry their subtree's totals at every level, processes included, so a folded row still shows what it hides.
- **Everything starts folded**: the app keeps the ids the user opened, not the ones folded, so the first draw shows only the projects and any row that appears later (a project, workspace, tab, pane or process) arrives folded, while what was opened stays open across readings and re-sorts. With many projects the unfolded tree was a wall of processes; a project's totals, bar and sparkline say where to look before opening it. A selected row hidden by folding one of its ancestors hands the selection to its nearest visible ancestor (the one just folded), not to the row now at its place, so the selection stays on what the user was looking at. The hot path is per pane (see [usage tree](usage-tree.md)) and marks every process from the pane's first one down, so a folded process on it stays yellow; panes and the levels above are not marked, since every pane has a hot path and all of them would light up. A double click and entering Context look for the Claude under the selected row's id, not under its visible children, so they work on a folded row. Rejected: unfolding down to the hot path on start, which brings back the wall with many panes.
- **Starting at the launch place**: run by a cornercase action (`CORNERCASE_CONTEXT` set, any value: `button` or the menu it came from), cctop takes the narrowest place the action names (`CORNERCASE_PANE`, then `_TAB`, `_WORKSPACE`, `_PROJECT`, the same rule as `cctop open`) and, on the first good reading, unfolds its ancestors only, selects it and scrolls it into view on the first draw; the row itself stays folded, as everything else (above). From the button that is what the window shows, so the popup opens on the pane the user was looking at, and entering Context marks its Claude (the usual rule for the selection). A place the tree does not hold is dropped, leaving the tree folded with nothing selected; a failed first reading keeps it for the next. Without `CORNERCASE_CONTEXT` (cctop in a split, or by hand in a shell) nothing is selected: a pane's own `CORNERCASE_PANE` would name the pane cctop runs in, which says nothing about what the user wants to see. Only the start uses it: later readings leave what the user folded alone.
- **Sorting from the UI**: the tree sorts by `Memory`, `Cpu` or nothing (cornercase's order). Clicking `Memory` or `CPU` sorts by it, clicking `Name` goes back to cornercase's order (marked on `Name`), `Agent`, `Status` and `Context` are not sortable and ignore the click. `s` cycles memory → CPU → cornercase's order. The config's `sort` maps onto the tree's sort in `app`, the only place that knows both. Changing it rebuilds the tree from the last reading, without waiting for a tick.
- **Keys**: `↑`/`↓` move the selection (from nothing, `↓` takes the first row and `↑` the last), `PgUp`/`PgDn` by a page, `Enter` toggles the fold, `←`/`→` fold and unfold, `Tab`/`Shift-Tab` switch views, `s` sorts, `q`, `Esc` and `Ctrl-C` quit (`Esc` cancels the kill dialog instead while it is open). The wheel scrolls three lines; moving the selection scrolls to keep it on screen. The list's keys, clicks and wheel only act on the Processes tab; on Context `↑`/`↓` and clicks move the mark among the agents, a click on a group or `Enter` folds groups, the wheel scrolls the groups and the tabs, `q`, `Esc` and `Ctrl-C` work, so the hidden list keeps its selection, folds, sort and scroll.
- **Errors**: a reading that fails (cornercase not running, `status` unparsable, the process table unreadable) shows its message in red on the top border of the list box, or of the conversation box in Context, and keeps the last good tree on screen; the next tick retries and clears it. A bad `config.toml` shows its first warning in the same place until the first key or click.
- **A hung `status`**: the cornercase client has no timeout, so the sampler thread is the only thing that can block on it; the UI loop only drains a channel and polls input every 250 ms, so it stays responsive and `q` quits at once (the sampler is not joined; a stuck `cornercase` child outlives cctop until it ends on its own). When no answer, good or bad, has arrived for three intervals (at least 5 s) the header says `cornercase has not answered for Ns`. Rejected: a timeout in `Runner` (a thread per call or a polling wait in the client) for a case that the thread already isolates.
- **Terminal**: alternate screen, raw mode and mouse capture are set on start and undone on any exit, including an error (a guard's `Drop`) and a panic (a hook that releases the mouse before ratatui's restores the screen).
